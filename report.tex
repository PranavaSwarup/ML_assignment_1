\begin{abstract}
This report documents the machine learning approach, mathematical formulation, and model selection methodology for developing polynomial regression models on two personalized geothermal datasets: Phase~1 (Steam Turbine Optimization, \texttt{var1}) and Phase~2 (Subterranean Thermal Reservoir Mapping, \texttt{var2}). Using stratified 5-fold cross-validation in \texttt{scikit-learn}, we exhaustively evaluated feature subsets, polynomial degree expansions, and regularization behavior. For Phase~1, the optimal architecture is a \textbf{Degree 4 polynomial using all 6 operational features} (210 terms, achieving a cross-validation MSE of $0.9618 \pm 0.1508$ and $R^2 = 0.9043 \pm 0.0189$). For Phase~2, the optimal architecture is a \textbf{Degree 8 polynomial using all 3 spatial coordinates} (165 terms, achieving a cross-validation MSE of $0.2523 \pm 0.0275$ and $R^2 = 0.9946 \pm 0.0008$). Both models resolve the bias-variance tradeoff and yield robust test-set predictions aligned with the underlying data generating processes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Problem Formulations}

Polynomial regression is a fundamental non-linear regression technique that projects raw input attributes into a higher-dimensional polynomial feature space while retaining a linear parameterization. In this assignment, we are tasked with developing polynomial regression models for two engineering problems in a multi-stage geothermal energy expansion project.

\subsection{Phase 1: Power Plant Steam Turbine Optimization (var1)}
Before drilling production wells, surface energy generation must be calibrated. Turbine efficiency is governed by six continuous operational control parameters ($x_1, \dots, x_6$), representing percentage deviations bounded in $[-1.0, 1.0]$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $x_1$: High-pressure steam valve adjustment
    \item $x_2$: Condenser coolant flow rate adjustment
    \item $x_3$: Re-injection pump hydraulic pressure
    \item $x_4$: Turbine blade pitch angle
    \item $x_5$: Non-condensable gas exhaust valve rate
    \item $x_6$: Steam inlet pressure adjustment
\end{itemize}
The target variable $y$ is the Net Power Score (training mean $0.8629$, standard deviation $3.1872$, range $[-9.879, 12.067]$). The problem statement specifies that the relationship is governed by a polynomial of moderate degree (up to degree 10).

\subsection{Phase 2: Subterranean Thermal Reservoir Mapping (var2)}
With the surface plant optimized, drilling coordinates must be selected by mapping subterranean geothermal anomalies. Seismic and thermal probe sensors record 3D spatial coordinate offsets from the basecamp landmark:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $x_1$: East-West coordinate offset
    \item $x_2$: North-South coordinate offset
    \item $x_3$: Vertical depth offset relative to basecamp
\end{itemize}
The target variable $y$ is the continuous Thermal Anomaly Score (training mean $2.2751$, standard deviation $6.9016$, range $[-30.257, 39.328]$). The heat map exhibits complex geological structures resembling a high-degree polynomial (up to degree 20).

\section{Mathematical Formulation and Validation Protocol}

\subsection{Polynomial Basis Expansion}
For an input feature vector $\mathbf{x} = [x_1, \dots, x_D]^T \in \mathbb{R}^D$, a polynomial regression model of degree $d$ is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
y = w_0 + \sum_{i=1}^D w_i x_i + \sum_{i=1}^D \sum_{j=i}^D w_{ij} x_i x_j + \dots = \boldsymbol{\Phi}(\mathbf{x})^T \mathbf{w} + \epsilon
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\mathbf{x})$ is the basis feature vector containing all monomials $\prod_{k=1}^D x_k^{p_k}$ such that the degree sum satisfies $\sum_{k=1}^D p_k \le d$. The total number of parameters (including bias) is given by the combinatorial combination with repetition:
\begin{equation}
P = \binom{D + d}{d} = \frac{(D + d)!}{D! \, d!}
\end{equation}

For Phase~1 ($D = 6$), the term count scales rapidly: degree 1 has 7 terms, degree 2 has 28 terms, degree 3 has 84 terms, degree 4 has 210 terms, and degree 5 has 462 terms. At degree 6, $P = 924$, approaching the sample size $N = 1000$. For Phase~2 ($D = 3$), scaling is substantially more compact: degree 4 has 35 terms, degree 6 has 84 terms, degree 8 has 165 terms, and degree 10 has 286 terms.

\subsection{Cross-Validation and Model Selection Metrics}
To avoid data leakage and prevent overfitting, we employed \textbf{5-Fold Cross-Validation} (\texttt{KFold(n\_splits=5, shuffle=True, random\_state=42)}). In each fold, 800 training observations were used to fit the parameters via Ordinary Least Squares (OLS), and 200 held-out samples evaluated prediction error. The evaluation metrics used are:
\begin{align}
\text{MSE} &= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 \\
R^2 &= 1 - \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \bar{y})^2}
\end{align}

\subsection{Rationale for Degree Selection in Phase 1}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Underfitting at Low Degrees ($d \le 3$):} A linear model ($d=1$) captures less than 10\% of response variance ($R^2 = 0.0937$). Quadratic modeling ($d=2$) captures 69.7\% but suffers substantial structural bias. Cubic modeling ($d=3$) achieves $R^2 = 0.8965$ with an MSE of $1.0438$.
    \item \textbf{Global Optimum at Degree 4:} Increasing to $d=4$ expands the basis to 210 terms, successfully capturing quaternary interaction dynamics. Validation MSE drops to its \textbf{global minimum of 0.9618}, while validation $R^2$ peaks at \textbf{0.9043}.
    \item \textbf{Overfitting at Degree 5 ($d \ge 5$):} With 462 terms, the model begins fitting sampling noise; training MSE drops to $0.1553$, while validation MSE sharply rises to $2.2071$ ($R^2 = 0.7787$).
    \item \textbf{Necessity of All 6 Features:} Combinatorial subset sweeps confirmed that all six operational features are critical. Restricting to any 3 features bounds $R^2 \le 0.5029$; restricting to 4 features bounds $R^2 \le 0.6274$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Rationale for Degree Selection in Phase 2}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Failure of Reduced Spatial Projections:} A single-coordinate model ($x_1$) swept up to degree 20 achieves an $R^2$ of only $0.0787$ (MSE $> 43.6$). A 2D slice ($x_1, x_2$) plateaus at $R^2 = 0.5925$. True subterranean thermal convection inherently requires all three spatial dimensions $(x_1, x_2, x_3)$.
    \item \textbf{Steep Error Reduction ($d = 4 \to 8$):} Increasing degree from 4 to 8 slashes validation MSE by over 93\% (from $3.9875$ to $0.2523$).
    \item \textbf{Global Optimum at Degree 8:} \textbf{Degree 8 achieves the absolute minimum cross-validation MSE ($0.2523 \pm 0.0275$)} and an $R^2$ of \textbf{0.9946}. The fold-to-fold error standard deviation reaches its lowest value ($\pm 0.0275$), confirming stability.
    \item \textbf{Onset of Overfitting ($d \ge 9$):} At degrees 9, 10, and 12, validation MSE systematically rises to $0.2954$, $0.3484$, and $1.0140$, respectively.
\end{enumerate}
